Hi-Lo card counting

Learn to count, one drill at a time.

Four drills that build on each other, from memorizing card values to converting a running count into the true count that signals your edge. When they feel automatic, take it to the Play tab and test it at a live table.

The drills

The order is deliberate. Card values have to be automatic before speed matters, and the running count has to be effortless before you layer the true-count division on top. Rushing ahead is the most common way people stall.

Pro move: once these feel easy, picture the card by its pips, not its printed number. Real dealers flash cards fast and you want to read a 5 as "+1" without reading the "5".

Drill 02

Deck Countdown

Tap through all 52 cards keeping your running count in your head. A full deck always nets to zero, so you must finish on whatever number you started from. Enter your final count and you'll know instantly whether you were perfect. Then try to go faster.

Starting from a number other than zero is how a real shoe looks mid-deck. You still finish on your start number, since a full deck nets to zero.

Benchmark: a sharp counter gets through a deck in about 20 seconds by hand. Don't chase that until you're hitting zero every single time. Accuracy first, speed second.

Drill 03

Speed Drill

Cards flash automatically at your chosen pace. Keep the running count, then enter it. This is the one that mimics the real tempo of a table.

Technique that helps here: cancellation. When a high and a low go by together, like a King and a 4, they wipe each other out, so you don't move the count at all. Spotting pairs that cancel is how you keep up when cards come fast.

Drill 04

True Count

A shoe of several decks deals out. Watch the discard tray fill to judge how many decks are left, keep the running count, then convert: running count divided by decks remaining equals true count.

Why bother dividing? A +8 running count means almost nothing with six decks still in the shoe, but with one deck left it's a +8 true count and a huge edge. The true count is what tells you when to raise your bet.

Drill 05 · the test

Live Table

Six-deck shoe, 3:2 blackjack, real dealing order. The running count ticks underneath every exposed card, including the dealer's hole card only once it's turned over, exactly as at a real table. Keep the count in your head, size your bets to it, and don't let the decisions knock you off.

Full table seats you with four others playing basic strategy. It burns the shoe faster and throws far more cards at you each round — harder, and closer to a real casino.

Reference

How Hi-Lo actually works

1. Every card has a tag

Low cards (2-6) are +1, middle cards (7-9) are 0, and high cards (10 through Ace) are −1. Low cards leaving the shoe is good for you, so they push the count up.

2. Keep a running count

Add every card's tag as it comes out. Start at zero at a fresh shuffle. A whole deck is balanced, so if you counted it all you'd land back on zero, which is exactly why the countdown drill can grade itself.

3. Convert to true count

Divide the running count by the number of decks still unplayed. Six decks are 312 cards, so if roughly 156 cards are gone, about three decks remain. A running count of +6 then becomes a true count of +2.

4. Act on the true count

A rising true count means the remaining cards favor you, so bet more. It also shifts a handful of strategy decisions, like taking insurance once the true count reaches about +3. The bet is where almost all of the advantage comes from.
